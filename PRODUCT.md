# University as a Service

## Lectures and access

- Students can browse courses and lectures, view lecture materials, rewatch lectures, and track their learning progress.
- During a lecture, signed-in students can interrupt by voice or text to ask contextual questions, request another explanation or example, and resume the lecture after the discussion. ElevenLabs provides voice capabilities.
- Universities can offer paid courses, courses restricted to eligible students, professors, and university admins, and open materials.
- The course sets default access. A university can make an individual lecture public even when its course is paid or restricted. The lecture and its attached materials share the lecture's visibility.
- Anyone can watch or rewatch public lectures and view their attached materials. Asking the AI lecturer a question requires an account.

## Notes and sharing

- Students can take and save private notes during a lecture. Notes can include timestamp links that jump to moments in the recording.
- In-app sharing requires group membership. The author chooses which members receive the note, either individually or through a group share to selected members. Once shared, access cannot be revoked by the author.
- A note can be exported as a PDF and read outside the group. Timestamp links in shared notes and PDFs follow the linked lecture's access rules.

## Revisions

- Authors can mark revisions **Draft** or **Ready**. A note stays private until the author shares it, even if it is Ready. Recipients can preview a draft explicitly shared with them.
- Each recipient must accept an update before it becomes their current version, even if the author has marked it Ready. One recipient's acceptance affects only their own view.
- Recipients can accept the latest shared revision directly, skip intermediate revisions, and reopen versions they previously accepted.

## Feedback

- Recipients can comment on specific passages in a shared draft. Each thread links to the passage in the revision where the comment was written.
- In a group share, the author and everyone included in the share can see and reply to threads. In an individual share, only the author and that recipient can see and reply. Group shares use shared feedback threads.
- The author can resolve a thread. The recipient who started it can reopen it. Resolved threads remain visible to the people who could already see them.
- Unresolved threads carry over to new revisions and retain their links to the original passages. If a passage is deleted, its unresolved thread stays visible in the new revision's feedback panel.
- Recipients can accept a revision while threads are open. Accepting the revision leaves those threads open.

## Decision history

| Decision | Alternatives discussed | Reason or outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allow lecture-level public access | Use the same visibility for the entire course | Universities can open an individual lecture and its materials within a restricted course. |
| Require sign-in for AI questions | Allow signed-out viewers to ask questions | Public viewing and AI interaction have different access requirements. |
| Share with selected group members | Share with the whole group | The author controls who receives each in-app share. |
| Require each recipient to accept a revision | Automatically display the latest Ready revision or use Fetch latest | Recipients can review a shared draft and decide when to adopt an update. |
| Carry unresolved threads forward | Keep threads visible only on the original revision | Reviewers can continue the discussion after the passage changes or is deleted. |
